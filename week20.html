🎟️ PLAYOFF TICKET PUNCHED

Team Lill Finally Slams The Door

Team Lill defeats Sarrel 566.87 – 496.47

Sarrel made Team Lill's heart flutter a few times this week, but in the end, Lill did what he needed to do: overpower Sarrel, secure the win, and officially cement his spot in the playoffs.

And with that, Moltisanti's Mistress's playoff dreams are officially dead. Team Lill didn't just close the door on Moltisanti — he gave those playoff hopes the Adriana treatment. One trip into the woods and they were never heard from again.

The funny part is that Team Lill damn near tried to whack his own playoff chances first.

Matthew Boyd dropped -33, Taj Bradley added -19, Grant Holmes chipped in -3, and Gavin Williams gave him another -12. Four pitchers actively trying to send their own manager to Cancun.

Luckily, the offense refused to participate in the sabotage. Corbin Carroll exploded for 84 points, Wilyer Abreu added 62, and Ketel Marte put up 42 to give Lill enough firepower to survive the attempted mutiny from his rotation.

And now the job is done. Team Lill is officially playoff bound. After spending what felt like half the season flirting with .500 and making his own life unnecessarily difficult, he has finally secured his seat at the table.

The question now is what version of this team shows up in the postseason. The talent is absolutely there, especially in the rotation, but when your guys are capable of dropping landmines like this combined with the Ketel Marte injury could make things interesting.

But those are playoff problems. For now, Lill gets to celebrate. The ticket is punched, Moltisanti is sleeping with the fishes, and the playoff field is set.

🧹 SEASON SWEEP

Wagner Reminds Justin Who His Daddy Is

Wagner defeats Justin 722.03 – 553.73

Justin's biggest competition in the standings and his biggest pest all season strikes again. Three matchups. Three Wagner victories. A clean 3–0 season sweep.

Wagner desperately needed the bounce-back performance we talked about in last week's recap, and of course it came against Justin. Because apparently no matter how Wagner is playing against the rest of the league, the moment Justin appears on the schedule this team suddenly remembers how to score, and score a fuck ton at that.

Justin actually won the battle at the plate, with his hitters outscoring Wagner's 253.4–219.9.

Unfortunately for Justin, Wagner's pitching staff then arrived with a fucking flamethrower.

Noah Cameron exploded for 101 points, Michael Soroka added 56, the Miz chipped in 40, and Cade Cavalli dropped another 70.

By the time the smoke cleared, Wagner had absolutely demolished Justin in the pitching department, 502.13–300.33.

And perhaps the funniest part of all this was watching something Justin once proudly described as his "SUPER BULLPEN" become his biggest liability of the week.

The Super Bullpen surrendered 14 home runs and 40 walks, while Kyle Harrison dropped -31.37, Will Warren added -28.9, and Brad Lord contributed -7.67. Less "Super Bullpen," more group of guys throwing batting practice with uniforms on.

And now we have to address the elephant in the room.

Justin simply cannot get over the Wagner hump. Three meetings this season and three losses. It hasn't mattered which team was hotter, which team was struggling, or what the standings looked like going in. When Wagner sees Justin on the other side, he owns him.

Of course, if you ask these two managers, none of this really matters yet. Both fully expect the next meeting to come in the championship.

Is the rest of the league quite as convinced that we're destined for a Wagner–Justin title game? Probably not. There are a few other playoff teams who may have something to say about that before these two start booking the venue.

But if their championship prophecy somehow does come true, Justin better pray the fourth time is the charm. Because right now a championship matchup against Wagner wouldn't look like Justin's dream scenario.

It would look like his final boss.

🏆 Playoff Tune-Up

Bryce Treats It Like October

Bryce defeats JohnnySack 777.80 – 685.47

JohnnySack knew his playoff spot was already secure and treated this week accordingly.

Multiple injured players stayed in the lineup, zero waiver claims were made, and the overall approach was pretty clear: kick the feet up, let the roster ride, and save the energy for when the games actually matter.

Bryce, meanwhile, treated this like championship weekend.

Four waiver claims, aggressive roster management, and a pitching staff that absolutely took control of the matchup. Bryce's rotation outscored JohnnySack's 541–472, and that ended up being the difference.

Bryce was powered by Logan Webb with 48, Logan Henderson with 60, Sonny Gray with 65, and an absurd 112.6 from Reid Detmers.

JohnnySack did not exactly roll over either. He got 43.2 from Walbert Ureña, 43.97 from Christian Scott, 56 from Payton Tolle, and 64 from Casey Mize.

But this one may have simply come down to volume. Bryce squeezed out 15 starts compared to JohnnySack's 11, and when the pitching production was already that strong, the extra opportunities were enough to push him over the top.

For both teams, though, the bigger picture matters more than this result. The playoff spots are locked, the regular-season pressure is basically over, and both managers are now looking ahead to the postseason.

And if the bracket breaks the right way, there is a very real chance these two see each other again. If that happens, JohnnySack probably will not be leaving injured players in the lineup and taking a nap the second time around.

⚰️ OFFICIALLY ELIMINATED

VinSalv Puts Moltisanti’s Mistress Out of His Misery

VinSalv defeats Moltisanti’s Mistress 624.07 – 606.93

Team Lill’s victory over Sarrel already slammed the door on Moltisanti’s Mistress’s playoff hopes. Apparently VinSalv wanted to make sure the door was locked, boarded shut, and the fucking house was condemned.

In a cruel bit of irony, Moltisanti didn’t just get eliminated from playoff contention — he lost to VinSalv and somehow fell BELOW HIM in the standings.

Yes. That VinSalv.

VinSalv is now 9–11. Moltisanti’s Mistress is 8–12.

Take a moment and let that sink in.

This was once one of the most promising rosters in the entire league. A lineup loaded with talent, bolstered by two of the best hitters in the American League, and for stretches of the season Moltisanti’s Mistress looked downright terrifying. There was a point where this team climbed all the way to #1 in the Power Rankings and looked capable of beating absolutely anybody.

Now?

8–12. Eliminated. And looking up at VinSalv in the standings.

Jesus Christ.

To rub salt in the wound even more, VinSalv didn't exactly execute some managerial masterclass to pull this off. He won a tight matchup while leaving more than 100 points sitting on his bench. Vin basically left the back door open, put the alarm code on the refrigerator, and told Moltisanti to come rob the place.

Moltisanti still couldn't get it done.

In fairness, first-year managers are going to take their lumps. Learning roster construction, waiver management, pitching volume, and how to survive a full fantasy baseball season isn't always easy. And early in the year, it genuinely looked like Moltisanti had figured it out ahead of schedule.

That's probably what makes the ending hurt so much more.

The talent was there. The ceiling was there. The midseason explosion was there. The wins just never came consistently enough, and now one of the league's most dangerous-looking teams will be watching the playoffs from the couch.

And having VinSalv be the guy standing over the body?

That's just disrespectful.

Pour one out.

RIP Moltisanti’s Mistress.
2026–2026.
Gone too soon. Probably should've won more games.

🗑️ THE CANCUN BOWL

Ice Wins. Jon Sucks at Managing a Fantasy Team. Moving On.

Both teams are eliminated from playoff contention, so we're not going to sit here and pretend this matchup had some massive impact on the league.

Ice scored 551.

Jon scored 287.

Two hundred and eighty-seven fucking points.

At some point we have to stop analyzing SlammingMuff's performances like there's some deeper baseball explanation hiding underneath all of this. There isn't.

Jon just sucks at managing a fantasy baseball team.

Good for Ice, though. He showed some legitimate signs of life during the second half of the season, and while the turnaround came too late to save his playoff hopes, at least he's finishing the year with a little dignity.

SlammingMuff, meanwhile, appears to have left his dignity on the waiver wire sometime around June.

551–287. Ice wins. Both teams are out. We've already spent too much time on this.

🔮 Two Weeks Remain

🏁 The Playoffs Are Set — The Seeding Isn't

The six playoff teams have officially punched their tickets, but with Weeks 21 and 22 still remaining, there is plenty left to settle.

The biggest thing to watch now is the race for playoff positioning. Bryce currently has the inside track, but JohnnySack and Team Lill still have two weeks to chase him down. Couple bad week from Bryce combined with a heater from either team could completely reshuffle the bracket before the postseason begins.

At the top, the battle for positioning continues as well. Justin and Wagner have spent most of the season fighting over league supremacy, and Wagner's latest win over Justin only adds another chapter to that rivalry heading toward the postseason. While both teams will get a bye, this is for bragging rights!

For the eliminated teams, the mission is much simpler: ruin somebody else's week. There may not be a playoff spot left to fight for, but there are still seeds to wreck, rivalries to settle, and two final opportunities to make somebody enter the playoffs pissed off.

Final Take:

Two weeks. That's it. The playoff field is set, but the bracket is not. By the end of Week 22, all the jockeying, shit talking, fraud allegations, and regular-season excuses are over. Then we find out who actually built a team capable of winning this thing.
