🏆 Matchup of the Week Recap

Team Lill Rips It Away From Wagner

Team Lill defeats Wagner 730.40 – 577.89

Wagner should officially be in panic mode.

Not because he is going to miss the playoffs — that ship has sailed. Wagner is locked in. The problem is what this team has looked like since losing to Ice back in Week 13.

Since then: beat SlammingMuff in Week 14 — big whoop. Lost to Moltisanti's Mistress in Week 15. Lost to JohnnySack in Week 16. Beat Sarrel in Week 17 — whooptie doo. Lost to Bryce in Week 18. Now loses to Team Lill in Week 19.

That makes Wagner just 2–5 over his last seven matchups, with his only two victories coming against teams that have spent most of the season forgetting they have fantasy baseball lineups.

The scary part? This matchup actually looked like Wagner was going to steal one.

Team Lill jumped out to a massive early advantage, leading 27.97 to -5.53 after Monday and stretching that lead to 157.04–86.67 by Tuesday night.

Wagner slowly started clawing his way back. By Wednesday, Lill's lead had shrunk to 244.34–199.67. By Thursday night, what had once been a comfortable advantage was basically gone: 290.24–289.47. Team Lill was holding onto the lead by just 0.77 points.

Then Friday happened.

Wagner outscored Team Lill 163.77–112.30 and finally took control, carrying a 453.24–402.54 lead into the weekend. After watching a huge advantage completely disappear, it looked like Team Lill had let another one slip away — and with the playoff race tightening, this would have been an absolutely brutal loss.

Then Team Lill fucking exploded.

Saturday: 155.53–82.90. Lead reclaimed.

Sunday: 172.33–41.77. Game over.

In two days, Team Lill turned a 50.70-point deficit into a 152.51-point victory. What looked like another heartbreaking Team Lill collapse instead became one of his biggest wins of the season.

Wagner's offense actually showed signs of life this week, with his hitters outscoring Team Lill's 341.8–310.5. The difference was entirely on the mound.

Wagner's pitchers looked like they had Team Lill jerseys underneath their uniforms. Jared Jones (-25.5), Davis Martin (-31.7), Robbie Ray (-4.5), and Justin Wrobleski (-42.57) did an unbelievable amount of damage to their own team.

The frustrating part for Wagner is that there were legitimate bright spots. Max Fried put up 47.07, JT Ginn added 58.8, and Noah Cameron delivered 63.4. Unfortunately, the double agents did far too much damage for those performances to matter.

Team Lill, meanwhile, finally looked like the roster we have been waiting to see all season. The talent showed up from top to bottom.

A Week 17 waiver claim in Jake McCarthy exploded for 84.6, Wilyer Abreu added 68.4, and Jonathan Aranda chipped in 44. On the mound, Grant Holmes dropped 73, while Cam Schlittler recovered for 50 points after looking like a complete pumpkin while lasting only three innings in his first start of the week.

When it was all said and done, Team Lill's pitchers demolished Wagner's 419.9–236.1. That was the matchup.

So is it full-on panic mode for Wagner? Maybe. The biggest problem is there is no cavalry coming. The trade deadline has passed, the roster is what it is, and Wagner now has to pray his own players stop actively working against him before the playoffs begin.

Luckily for Wagner, next week brings exactly the opponent he may need: Team Justin.

Does Wagner lose to Ice? Yes. Does Wagner lose to contenders? Apparently. Does Wagner lose to Justin? ABSOLUTELY NEVER.

Wagner has owned Justin all season, and now gets an opportunity to complete the sweep. If there was ever a matchup capable of snapping this team out of its funk and rebuilding some confidence before the postseason, this might be it.

Either Wagner gets right against his favorite punching bag... or the fraud sirens are about to become deafening.

💔 Playoff Hopes on Life Support

Justin May Have Just Buried Moltisanti’s Mistress

Justin defeats Moltisanti’s Mistress 752.90 – 701.93

This one hurts.

What once looked like one of the most talented and dangerous teams in the league may have just watched its entire season go down the drain. Moltisanti’s Mistress puts up 701.93 points — a score that would have beaten 77.8% of the league this week — and somehow walks away with a loss.

And Justin damn near looked like he was trying to keep Moltisanti’s playoff hopes alive.

Justin left an injured Nick Kurtz sitting in his active lineup all week, essentially playing a man down and daring Moltisanti to take advantage. He couldn't.

For the second time in these Week 19 recaps, the losing team actually won the battle at the plate. Moltisanti’s Mistress outscored Justin’s hitters 307.8–270.

But just like in real baseball, pitching wins games — and that is exactly where Justin took this one.

Both rotations were nearly flawless. There were no massive blowups, no catastrophic implosions, and almost no negative performances anywhere. Justin's only real pitching miscue was Ranger Suárez at -0.9, while Moltisanti somehow made it through the entire week without a single pitcher finishing in the negative.

It still wasn't enough.

Justin's rotation outscored Moltisanti's 482.9–394.13, getting 54 from Gerrit Cole, 51 from Kyle Harrison, and 42 from Framber Valdez. There was no single nuclear performance — just relentless production across the board.

Moltisanti certainly had his chances. Drew Rasmussen put up 56, Eduardo Rodriguez added 72, and then there was 72.9 from THE SAMOAN SOUTHPAW, Sean Manaea.

Yes, that Sean Manaea.

The same pitcher Moltisanti has spent an unreasonable amount of time telling anyone who will listen absolutely sucks. Since July 24, Moltisanti's mortal enemy has pitched to a 2.16 ERA and is now desperately trying to save the season of the man who refuses to believe in him. You cannot write this shit.

But I digress.

Even with Manaea dealing, a strong rotation behind him, and Junior Caminero launching three home runs and putting up 44 points, Moltisanti simply ran into the wrong opponent at the wrong time.

A 701.93-point week would have beaten everyone in the league except Team Lill and Justin. Unfortunately, Moltisanti happened to be playing Justin.

And with that, Justin may have delivered the final blow to Moltisanti’s playoff hopes.

May have.

Because there is still one tiny, ridiculous path back from the dead. This all assumes Team Lill handles Sarrel this week. If Team Lill somehow manages to lose to a team that has spent the season handing out wins like Halloween candy, the door cracks back open for Moltisanti to make one last miracle run. Moltisanti also gets a ridiculously easy end to the season with his next 3 games against VinSalv, Ice, and SlammingMuff.

Is it likely? Absolutely not.

But this league has already produced enough stupid shit this season that I'm not declaring anybody mathematically dead until somebody puts the body in the ground.

For now, though, Justin gets the win — and Moltisanti’s Mistress is officially one Sarrel upset away from needing a miracle.

😴 Just Taking the Week Off?

Bryce Survives Sarrel... Somehow

Bryce defeats Sarrel 657.33 – 608.37

Was Bryce just not trying this week?

That's about the only explanation I can come up with, because how the hell do you end up winning the Closest Matchup of the Week against Sarrel of all teams?

I'm going to give Bryce the benefit of the doubt here. Maybe he saw Sarrel on the schedule, put the phone down, kicked his feet up, and decided this was his bye week.

And honestly? He's earned the opportunity to relax a little. Bryce has quietly become one of the hottest teams in the entire league and has now rattled off six straight wins.

But I'm hoping that's what happened, because if Bryce was actually trying his hardest and only managed 657.33 against Sarrel, there might be a tiny reason for concern. One mediocre week means nothing. Two or three and we'll start asking questions.

As for Sarrel?

Same shit, different week.

Sarrel left another 133 points sitting on his bench. In a matchup he lost by only 48.96 points.

That's right. Sarrel actually had more than enough production sitting on the bench to pull off the upset, hand Bryce his first loss in six weeks, and potentially shake up the standings.

Instead, he gave it away.

And quite frankly, this one personally pisses me off because a Bryce loss would've given me an opportunity to catch him in the standings.

So thank you, Sarrel. Very cool.

Bryce survives, the winning streak reaches six, Sarrel leaves another mountain of points on the bench, and somehow Bryce vs. Sarrel walks away with the Closest Matchup Award.

I have nothing else to say about this shit. On to the next.

🏖️ Took the Week Off

JohnnySack Collects His Free Win

JohnnySack defeats Ice 597.70 – 418.90

Remember everything I just accused Bryce of doing against Sarrel?

Yeah... I did the exact same shit.

JohnnySack saw Ice on the schedule, kicked his feet up, made zero waiver claims, and decided there was absolutely no reason to get cute this week.

Byron Buxton and Samuel Basallo spent the entire week sitting in JohnnySack's active lineup despite both being on the IL. No replacements. No scrambling. No unnecessary roster gymnastics. Just let it ride and collect the free win.

And somehow, we have yet another matchup where the losing team actually won the battle at the plate.

Ice's hitters outscored JohnnySack's 305–253.

Unfortunately for Ice, fantasy baseball requires pitchers too.

JohnnySack absolutely demolished Ice on the mound, 344–113. That is a 231-point difference from the rotations alone.

And the explanation is pretty simple: 14 pitchers used by JohnnySack. 9 by Ice.

That's really all you need to know.

Ice knows his season is over and has clearly stopped trying to squeeze every last point out of this roster. JohnnySack recognized that, decided there was no reason to burn waiver claims or mess with his lineup, and essentially treated Week 19 like a scheduled rest day.

The result wasn't pretty. 597.70 points is nothing to write home about. But at this point in the season, style points don't matter.

JohnnySack showed up with two injured players in his lineup, made zero claims, got outscored at the plate, failed to crack 600... and still won by 178.80 points.

Sometimes the schedule hands you a free one. You say thank you, take the win, and move the fuck on.

🗑️ Managerial Malpractice

VinSalv Defeats SlammingMuff in a Matchup Nobody Asked For

VinSalv defeats SlammingMuff 449.47 – 314.27

Do we even need to recap this one?

Probably not. But since we're here, let's highlight a recurring theme with these two teams.

The rosters aren't necessarily the problem. The managers are.

VinSalv left an absolutely disgusting 318.43 points on his bench this week. Yes, you read that correctly. 318.43. He nearly had as many points sitting on the bench as SlammingMuff scored in the entire matchup.

And don't worry, Jon wasn't about to be outdone in the managerial malpractice department. SlammingMuff left another 125.43 points on his bench.

That's 443.86 combined bench points between these two teams.

The funniest part is neither roster is completely devoid of talent. There are useful players here. There are points available. There are moves that could be made.

Their managers just suck.

VinSalv wins by 135.20 points, so congratulations to him, I guess. I'm not wasting any more time analyzing a 449–314 fantasy baseball matchup.

Moving the fuck on.

🔮 Week 17 Preview:

🔥 Matchup of the Week

Bryce @ Moltisanti's Mistress

This is the clear matchup of the week.

Bryce enters riding a three-game winning streak and sits comfortably at 10–6, while Moltisanti's Mistress suddenly finds himself back at .500 (8–8) after last week's stunning upset loss to Sarrel.

Just one week ago Moltisanti was sitting atop the Power Rankings after looking like the hottest team in the league. One bad week doesn't erase that, but another loss suddenly puts him in an uncomfortable spot with the playoff pack closing in.

Bryce, meanwhile, has quietly silenced the fraud talk. Three straight wins and a 921-point explosion last week have him looking every bit like a legitimate contender again.

Two teams with very different momentum. One massive game with huge playoff implications.

VinSalv @ JohnnySack

JohnnySack has completed one of the best turnarounds of the season, climbing from dead last all the way to 9–7 and fourth place.

VinSalv, meanwhile, desperately needs a win to keep his playoff hopes alive. The talent is there, but the consistency has not been.

JohnnySack has looked like one of the league's best teams over the past two months. Another win would continue his remarkable climb up the standings.

Ice @ Justin

Justin finally reclaimed sole possession of first place, but nobody is confusing this team with the juggernaut we saw earlier this season.

The offense has cooled considerably, and while the wins are still coming, the dominant performances have disappeared.

Ice has been one of the unluckiest teams in the league over the past month. If he catches even one break this week, don't be shocked if he gives Justin everything he can handle.

Sarrel @ Wagner

Wagner has now dropped back-to-back games and has failed to crack 620 points for five straight weeks.

Fortunately for him, the schedule gives him what appears to be the perfect opportunity to get right against Sarrel.

Of course... we said the exact same thing about Moltisanti last week. Wagner cannot afford to overlook anybody right now.

SlammingMuff @ Team Lill

Team Lill climbed back to 8–8 after surviving Ice and remains firmly in the playoff hunt.

SlammingMuff's season is hanging by a thread. At 6–10, another loss would all but slam the door shut on any miracle comeback.

Team Lill has to take care of business. The margin for error is shrinking with every passing week.

Final Take:

Week 17 is all about momentum.

Bryce looks to continue his surge, Moltisanti's Mistress tries to bounce back, JohnnySack attempts to keep climbing, and both Justin and Wagner are searching for convincing wins after a month of underwhelming baseball.

The playoff picture is beginning to separate itself, and every matchup now feels like it could decide somebody's season.
